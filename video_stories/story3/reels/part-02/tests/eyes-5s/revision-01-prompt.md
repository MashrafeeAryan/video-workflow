## Story context

This is episode 2, The Decision, continuing the approved Settler Rush betrayal

## Exact action and camera prompt

Vertical 9:16, grounded photorealistic historical science-fiction cinematography.
Keep the first preview's approved lighting and material treatment: natural skin
detail, worn olive linen and rust-red scarf, amber firelight, muted slate-blue
shadows, restrained performance and subtle film texture. Eli is the same lean
17-year-old boy with warm brown skin, short untidy black hair and dark brown eyes.

ONE continuous five-second OVERHEAD shot. Eli is already LYING FLAT ON HIS BACK
on level dusty cobblestones before the first frame. The back of his head, both
shoulder blades and his upper back stay supported by those stones for the entire
shot. His face points UP toward the sky. The camera is directly ABOVE him and
its lens points STRAIGHT DOWN, perpendicular to the ground, throughout.

0–1.5 seconds: extreme close-up of his CLOSED eyes from directly overhead. Begin
a slow vertical crane rise. The camera physically travels UP away from his face;
Eli does not move toward the camera. Keep the lens pointed straight down.

1.5–3 seconds: as the camera continues rising, his whole face comes into view.
He opens both eyes naturally once, looking upward at the lens. His head and
shoulders remain resting against the ground; only eyelids and breathing move.

3–5 seconds: the continuing rise reveals his hair against the stones, the entire
head and both shoulders, olive shirt and red scarf spread on the ground. Show
cobblestones around BOTH sides of his head and shoulders, establishing that his
body is horizontal. End overhead, with his face smaller in frame than at the
start. At the frame edges, smoke, faint embers and soft orange reflections imply
the nearby burning village. Keep him away from flames and unharmed.

Quiet breathing, distant fire crackle and wind only. No dialogue, music or text.

## Edge-case / negative direction

No standing, sitting, crouching, propping on elbows, raising head or shoulders,
getting up, running, or portrait-style upright framing. No camera lowering to
eye level, tilting toward the horizon or rotating the boy upright. No buildings
stacked behind an upright head: the immediate background around his head is the
ground beneath him. No abrupt cut, reverse zoom, face morph, extra person,
unrequested blink cycle, injuries, blood, subtitle, logo, title or endcard.

If Flow only supports eight-second sources, perform the complete above action
within seconds 0–5, hold the established overhead composition during 5–8, and
trim a separate five-second scene in Flow. This does not authorize more spending.

## Review gates

At the end, both shoulders and the back of his head must visibly rest on the
ground; the ground must surround him. A realistic face alone does not pass.
Check initial closed eyes, one natural opening, same face, continuous upward
camera travel, fixed downward angle, five-second duration and no getting up.
If a correctly posed still is used as a generation reference, inspect it before
spending on animation. The earlier upright preview can guide appearance only.
